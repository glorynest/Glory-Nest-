7 Bible Verses About Anxiety and Worry

Worry can make tomorrow feel heavy before it even arrives. When responsibilities, uncertainty, or difficult circumstances fill our minds, taking a quiet moment with Scripture can help us reflect on God's presence and remember that we do not have to face every challenge alone.

These seven Bible passages offer encouragement for anxious moments. Read each one slowly, consider its meaning, and choose one small action you can take today.

1. Philippians 4:6–7 — Bring Your Worries to God

“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.”

Philippians 4:6, KJV

What it means: This passage invites believers to bring their concerns to God through honest prayer. It does not mean that problems are imaginary or that anxious feelings disappear instantly. It encourages us to make room for prayer and gratitude while we face what is difficult.

Try this today: Write down one concern, pray about it, and identify one practical step within your control.

2. 1 Peter 5:7 — Cast Your Cares on Him

“Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.”

1 Peter 5:7, KJV

What it means: This verse reminds Christians that their struggles matter to God. You do not have to pretend to be strong every moment or hide what is troubling you.

Try this today: Name the burden you are carrying. Share it in prayer, and consider talking with someone trustworthy who can support you.

3. Matthew 6:34 — Take One Day at a Time

“Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself.”

Matthew 6:34, KJV

What it means: Jesus teaches his listeners not to let tomorrow's uncertainty consume today's attention. Planning can be wise, but repeatedly imagining every possible problem can leave us exhausted before we begin.

Try this today: Instead of solving the whole week at once, choose one useful task to complete today.

4. Isaiah 41:10 — Remember That You Are Not Alone

“Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God.”

Isaiah 41:10, KJV

What it means: These words offer reassurance of God's presence and strength during fearful seasons. Courage does not require never feeling afraid; sometimes it means continuing one step at a time while seeking help and support.

Try this today: When fear rises, pause, breathe slowly, and remind yourself of one source of strength or support available to you.

5. Psalm 55:22 — You Can Seek Help With Your Burdens

“Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee.”

Psalm 55:22, KJV

What it means: This psalm expresses trust in God's sustaining care. It can remind readers that hardship is not something they must carry in isolation.

Try this today: Identify one burden you can share with a trusted friend, family member, faith leader, or qualified professional.

6. John 14:27 — Receive Christ's Words of Peace

“Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you.”

John 14:27, KJV

What it means: Jesus offers his disciples a kind of peace rooted in his presence and teaching. This promise is not a demand to feel calm all the time. It can be a source of hope while we work through uncertainty.

Try this today: Set aside five quiet minutes to read this passage, reflect on it, and let your thoughts settle without judging yourself.

7. Psalm 34:4 — Seek God When You Feel Afraid

“I sought the LORD, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.”

Psalm 34:4, KJV

What it means: David describes turning to God in a time of fear. His words encourage believers to seek God honestly, even when they feel vulnerable or uncertain.

Try this today: Pray in your own words about what frightens you. You do not need perfect words to express an honest concern.

A Simple Prayer for Anxiety and Worry

Heavenly Father, when my thoughts feel heavy and the future seems uncertain, help me remember that I am not alone. Give me wisdom for the decisions before me, courage for the challenges I cannot avoid, and patience to take one day at a time. Help me recognize the support around me and respond to my worries with faith, care, and practical action. Amen.

What Can You Do When Worry Returns?

  • Read one of these passages slowly and reflect on its message.
  • Write down what is troubling you and separate what you can change from what you cannot control.
  • Take a small practical step, such as making a plan, resting, or asking for help.
  • Talk to a trusted person if your worries feel difficult to manage.

Faith and practical support can work together. If anxiety is persistent, overwhelming, or affecting daily life, consider speaking with a qualified mental health professional. Seeking help is not a failure of faith.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good Bible verse to read when feeling anxious?

Philippians 4:6–7 is a helpful place to begin because it encourages prayer and describes God's peace. You may also find comfort in 1 Peter 5:7 and Isaiah 41:10.

Does the Bible say that Christians will never feel anxious?

No single verse promises that believers will never experience fear or anxiety. Scripture offers encouragement, wisdom, prayer, and hope for difficult seasons.

How can I use Bible verses in my daily routine?

Choose one short passage each morning, write down what it means to you, and consider one practical way to apply its message during the day.
