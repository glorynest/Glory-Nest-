Bible Verse · Hope and Renewal

Isaiah 43:18–19 — God Is Doing a New Thing

“Forget the former things; do not dwell on the past. See, I am doing a new thing!”
Isaiah 43:18–19

A moment to reflect

There are seasons when yesterday's disappointments make it hard to imagine a hopeful tomorrow. Isaiah 43:18–19 invites us to notice God's work and trust that hardship does not have to be the end of the story. The passage does not ask us to deny pain or pretend the past did not happen. It calls us to remember God's faithfulness and remain open to the way He leads us forward.

The context of Isaiah 43:18–19

Isaiah 43 is part of a message of comfort and hope to the people of Israel. They had experienced loss and the threat of exile, and they needed reassurance that God had not abandoned them. Earlier in the chapter, God reminds them of His power and of the way He made a path through the sea when He brought their ancestors out of Egypt.

When God says He is doing a new thing, He points His people toward a fresh act of deliverance and restoration. The image of a road in the wilderness and rivers in a dry land shows that God can provide a way where people see no way. In its original setting, this was a promise to His people; it should not be reduced to a guarantee that every personal plan will happen exactly as we wish.

What does “forget the former things” mean?

It does not mean erasing memories, ignoring injustice, or refusing to learn from past experiences. It means not allowing former troubles—or even former victories—to keep our attention from what God is doing now. We can remember with gratitude, grieve honestly, learn wisely, and still move forward with faith.

What is the “new thing” God is doing?

In this passage, the new thing refers to God's promised work of rescue and restoration for Israel. The wilderness road and desert rivers are pictures of His ability to sustain and guide His people in an impossible situation. For readers today, this passage can encourage trust in God's character and attentiveness to His guidance, even when the next step is not yet clear.

How to apply this verse to your life

  • Be honest about the past. Name what has hurt you or what you still need to learn, without letting it define your whole identity.
  • Remember God's faithfulness. Reflect on moments when you received help, strength, comfort, or guidance.
  • Ask for wisdom about the next step. Pray, read Scripture, and seek advice from trustworthy people.
  • Take one practical action. Begin a healthy habit, repair a relationship where appropriate, ask for support, or make a responsible plan.
  • Allow hope to grow gradually. A new season may begin with a small step rather than an immediate change in circumstances.

A real-life example

Imagine someone who has faced repeated setbacks and has started believing that nothing can improve. This verse can remind them that their past does not reveal every possibility ahead. They might begin by asking for help, making a realistic plan, and taking one small step each day. Their circumstances may not change overnight, but they can move forward with renewed hope and trust in God's guidance.

Questions for personal reflection

  1. What past experience is making it difficult for me to hope?
  2. What can I learn from that experience without allowing it to control my future?
  3. Where might I need God's guidance, patience, or courage right now?
  4. What is one small, wise step I can take this week?

A prayer for new beginnings

Heavenly Father, thank You for remaining faithful through every season of my life. Help me face my past honestly, learn what I need to learn, and release the weight I cannot carry alone. When the way ahead feels uncertain, give me patience to listen for Your guidance and courage to take the next right step. Renew my hope and help me recognize Your care even in difficult places. Lead me in Your wisdom each day. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
